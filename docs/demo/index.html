Orca demo

Open Replayable Constraint-based Analysis

Walkthrough: how it works, current results Code on GitHub

Type an English sentence and get a complete grammar analysis with its meaning (MRS).

  1. 1
    Label

    A neural tagger gives every word a label, and the labels decode to a full derivation. This is parsing as sequence labeling.

  2. 2
    Replay

    The English Resource Grammar (Flickinger 2000) checks the derivation by replay and repairs it where it fails.

  3. 3
    Rank

    The grammar’s ranking model picks among the accepted derivations.

The idea behind the parser

Parsing as sequence labeling comes from our group, LyS at Universidade da Coruña: Carlos Gómez-Rodríguez and David Vilares, Constituent parsing as sequence labeling (EMNLP 2018). Orca applies it to HPSG derivations and lets the grammar check every result.

Measured

Built on DELPH-IN

Orca is inspired by DELPH-IN and builds on that community’s many years of work, including the English Resource Grammar (Flickinger 2000) and the ACE parser (Crysmann and Packard 2012) used on this page.

About

An orca is a fast dolphin, although not the fastest. Orca is developed with Claude. The code is under the MIT License, given in full at the bottom of this page.

Ctrl+Enter parses

Partial analysis

The grammar accepts no analysis of the whole sentence. These are the largest pieces of the tagger’s first guess that it does accept, each with its own meaning. They are not an analysis of the sentence and are never counted as one.

Readings

The sentence has more than one reading the grammar accepts and the ranking model finds close. Choose one to see its meaning, derivation and feature structure.

Meaning

Predicates and the links between them (dashed links are scope).

MRS

The meaning as MRS: one row per predicate, ordered by the words it comes from. Click a variable to see everywhere it occurs and what is known about it; click a predicate or a word to link the two.

Derivation

      
Purple boxes are words, with their lexical type (gray) and lexical rules (purple). Blue boxes are syntactic rules; a box with several names is a chain of unary rules.

      

    


    

      
Feature structure

      
The whole sign the grammar built. A folded value shows a summary of what is inside; click it to unfold one level, ▾ to fold. Click a feature name to show only that value (the path above leads back). Values with nothing specified (bool, luk …) are hidden unless you ask. Purple numbers mark shared values; click one to light up where it occurs. A path such as SYNSEM LOCAL CAT HEAD opens directly. With ACE's comparison on, orange marks values that differ from ACE's sign.
