Orca: the road to high coverage and speed

Every stage runs the same loop: the tagger proposes, the grammar checks, what fails is repaired, the accepted analyses become training data, and the result is measured. The stages differ in what feeds the loop: first more and better data for English, then a larger grammar, then data at a scale only a fast parser can make, then other languages. The stages overlap; each later one starts while the earlier one is still improving.

The loop inside every stage

  1. Propose. The tagger labels each word; the labels fix one complete derivation.
  2. Check. The grammar replays the derivation. Accepted means licensed by the grammar, with full structure and MRS.
  3. Repair. On rejection, the tagger's next-best labels are tried under the failing node; the grammar's ranking model picks among accepted analyses. What remains goes to the LLM step offline, never at parse time.
  4. Learn. Accepted analyses of new text become training data; accepted LLM repairs become repair patterns and training examples (never from the development or test sets).
  5. Measure. Same meaning as gold, any analysis, partial meaning (F1), and sentences per second, on the same held-out sentences each time; the walkthrough page is updated.

Sentences the grammar cannot license at all leave the loop (dashed arrow) and become the input of stage 2.

Four stages

STAGE 1 · NOW

A fast parser for English

The English Resource Grammar, parsed fast enough to analyze large corpora, with the accuracy of the chart parser ACE.

Goes in

  • Redwoods treebank (ERG 2025)
  • ACE parses of a literary corpus and a news corpus (human and LLM-written text), checked by replay
  • The grammar's own ranking model

Steps

  • doneReplay engine in C; MRS extraction (gold derivations give the gold MRS for every dev sentence)
  • doneTagger, repair, ranking: 67.2% exact meaning, 81.1% with an analysis, 12.6 sentences per second on one core (ACE: 85.7%, 1.0)
  • doneLLM repair measured; role set to offline
  • doneGrammar parses of a literary and a news corpus checked by replay and used for training
  • doneSilver training data: best so far 73.3% exact and 88.7% with an analysis (both corpora, human and LLM-written)
  • nextRepeat the best runs with other random seeds, to know what differences are real
  • nextRepair patterns learned from accepted LLM repairs
  • nextLong sentences: join the subtrees that replay instead of failing the whole sentence
  • nextTokenization from raw text (now taken from the treebank)
  • laterFaster repair (the remaining cost is orchestration, not unification); one final run on the test set

Comes out

  • A parser that can make silver data at scale
  • The list of sentences no derivation of this grammar covers
Gate to stage 3 (proposed): exact meaning and coverage close to ACE on the development set, end to end clearly faster than ACE, on long sentences too.
STAGE 2

Grammar expansion

Grow the grammar where real text shows gaps, with every change checked against everything the grammar already does.

Goes in

  • Sentences neither Orca nor ACE can analyze (in the literary corpus alone, over 4,000 have no ACE analysis)
  • Replay failure reports: the node, the feature path, the clashing types
  • The grammar's definitions (TDL), read by an LLM
  • Review by the grammar's maintainers

Steps

  • nextLLM diagnosis: why the grammar rejects the closest analysis, from the definitions and the failure point
  • laterProposed changes (lexical entries first, then types and rules), each tested by replay
  • laterRegression check: every treebank derivation still replays to its gold meaning
  • laterRelaxed analyses for what the grammar still cannot license, labeled as such

Comes out

  • New grammar versions with higher coverage
  • A record of the grammar's gaps by phenomenon
Feeds back: each new grammar version means re-checking the training data by replay and retraining (stage 1 loop).
STAGE 3

Data at scale

Use the fast parser to make the training data that ACE is too slow to make, and to serve corpus studies.

Goes in

  • Large raw corpora, several genres
  • The stage 1 parser and the stage 2 grammar
  • The LLM step, offline, for sentences the parser cannot analyze

Steps

  • laterParse large corpora with Orca; keep only analyses the grammar accepts
  • laterSelf-training rounds, each measured on the same held-out sentences
  • laterSpot checks by annotators; held-out parts of each corpus
  • laterCheck that rare constructions keep their frequencies, for the human versus LLM text studies

Comes out

  • Millions of grammar-licensed analyses
  • Parses for corpus studies (literary and news comparisons)
Watch: the parser learning its own mistakes. Each round must improve the held-out measures, or it is discarded.
STAGE 4

Other languages

The same loop for other grammars and languages, starting where grammars and treebanks exist.

Goes in

  • The Spanish Resource Grammar and its treebanks first
  • Other DELPH-IN grammars
  • Universal Dependencies treebanks, through a bridge
  • Grammar descriptions, for LLM-assisted grammar building

Steps

  • laterSpanish: encode the treebanks, train, replay, repair
  • laterOne tagger across languages, with a label set per grammar
  • laterBridge to UD, in both directions
  • laterInspectable grammars from other sources and theories

Comes out

  • Fast parsing with full structure and semantics beyond English
Depends on: stage 1 working without English-specific shortcuts (tokenization, lexicon lookup, ranking).

How the stages feed each other

Grammar to parserEvery grammar change (stage 2) re-checks the training data by replay and starts a retraining round (stage 1).
Data to parserEvery batch of new analyses (stage 3) is a retraining round, kept only if the held-out measures improve.
Parser to grammarEvery round leaves sentences the grammar cannot license: the input of the next grammar expansion.
